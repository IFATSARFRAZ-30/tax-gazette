The Finance Act 2026 has been notified for tax year 2026-27, which means the changes announced in the budget are now law. The revised salaried apply from 1 July 2026, so they affect the salary you receive now, not only the return you will file next year.

The Act also changes the and the rates of . Salaried people should look at the slabs first, because they decide how much tax is deducted from each payslip. Business owners should read the withholding and super tax provisions, since those change what is deducted from their payments and what is due at the end of the year.

Finance Act 2026: what changed for high earners

Surcharge and super tax changes, with links to the Act.

By the Gazette desk·15 July 2026

The Finance Act 2026 changes the and the that apply to higher incomes. Both are charged on top of ordinary income tax, which is why a change to either one alters your total bill even when the normal slabs stay the same.

A surcharge is worked out as a percentage of the income tax you already owe, while super tax is worked out on income, at rates that depend on the income level. If your income sits near the edge of a band, a small change in these rules can matter more than a change in the slabs themselves, so it is worth checking where you now fall.

Our full story sets out each change and links to the Act, so you can read the wording for yourself. The calculator on the Tools page covers the salaried slabs only and does not add the surcharge or super tax, so a high earner's real bill will be higher than its estimate.

Withholding tax

Why non-filers pay more

What Active Taxpayers List status changes at the bank, on property and on vehicles.

By the Gazette desk·Explainer

Filing your return is what puts you on the , and that status decides the rates you face through the year. A person who is not on the list is charged higher rates on the same transaction, so the cost of not filing is paid in small amounts each time you use the bank, buy property or register a vehicle.

The higher rates are deducted at the time of the transaction by the bank, the registrar or the seller, so you feel the cost straight away. Most people only notice the difference when they compare a deduction with what a filer paid on a similar transaction.

The remedy is simple: file your return and confirm that your name appears on the list. The list can take a short while to update after you file, so do not leave it to the last week of the deadline.

Salaried taxpayers

What to check on your payslip after the new slabs

Your employer deducts tax every month. Here is how to tell whether the deduction looks right.

By the Gazette desk·Guide

Every month your employer deducts income tax from your salary and pays it to the government on your behalf. This is an advance against your tax for the whole year, and it is worked out using the salaried . Because the revised slabs apply from 1 July 2026, the tax line on your payslip may differ from what it was in the previous tax year.

The simplest check is to take your monthly salary, enter it in the salary tax calculator and compare the monthly tax it shows with the tax line on your payslip. The two will not always match to the rupee, because your payslip may include allowances, bonuses or deductions that the calculator does not know about, but a large gap is a reason to ask questions.

If the difference looks too big, ask your payroll or accounts department which slabs they used and from which month. Keep every payslip, because the total tax deducted over the year is the figure you will rely on when you prepare your return.

Filing guide

Documents to keep ready before you file your return

A short checklist that saves time when you sit down to file on IRIS.

By the Gazette desk·Guide

Most of the time spent filing a return goes into looking for papers, not into the filing itself. If you collect your documents first, the actual entry on the portal is much faster and you are less likely to leave something out.

Start with your income: salary slips or an annual salary certificate from your employer, and records of any other income such as rent, profit or business receipts. Then gather the tax already deducted from you, which is covered by the statements and certificates you receive from employers and banks. Finally, collect details of what you own and owe, such as property, vehicles, bank accounts and loans, because the return asks you to declare your assets and liabilities.

Check that your identity and contact details with are current before you start. A return filed with the right documents in front of you is easier to defend later if anyone asks a question about it.

How to follow new SROs without reading every notification

A short guide to what an SRO is, why it matters and how our tracker helps.

By the Gazette desk·Guide

An is a notice issued under powers given by a tax law. It is how rules are changed between budgets: an SRO can grant or withdraw an exemption, change a rate for a particular class of goods or people, or set out a procedure. Because they are issued throughout the year, a rule that applied last month may not apply today.

Reading the original notification is difficult for most people, because it is written to fit exactly into the language of the law it amends. The useful questions are plain ones: which tax does it concern, who does it affect, what changes, and from when.

The SRO Tracker lists recent orders by tax type. Open any order to see a plain-English explanation of what it does, and use the link inside it to read the original PDF for the exact wording.

Withholding tax

Keep your withholding tax statements, they are worth money

Tax deducted from you in advance can be counted against your final bill, but only if you can show it.

By the Gazette desk·Guide

When a bank, an employer or another payer deducts from you, that amount is usually credited against the tax you owe for the year, so it is not lost. In some cases the deduction is treated as the final tax on that income, and in others it is only an advance that you adjust in your return. Either way, the deduction is only useful to you if you can prove it was made.

Payers are normally required to give you a statement or certificate showing what was deducted. Collect these as they arrive, such as the annual statement from your bank or the certificate from your employer, and file them together with your payslips.

When you prepare your return, add up the tax deducted from all sources and enter it in the return. If you cannot find a statement, ask the payer for a copy before the filing deadline, because a payer may take some time to issue it.
